BOSS: JUMP-TO-CONCLUSIONS

LEVEL UP!

Say: Amy thinks to herself, "Wait, am I overthinking this? I don't know for sure that Nicole is ignoring me. Maybe she didn't see me. I'll say hi and see what happens."

Let the class choose Amy's move from the menu (↑ ↓ then Enter, or click).

Amy says, "Hi Nicole". Nicole replies, "Oh hi Amy. I didn't see you. I saved a spot for you. I'd like you to meet my friend Brittany."

What were Nicole's intentions at lunchtime? (To have lunch with Amy. To introduce Amy to Brittany.)

How do we know this? (She saved a place for Amy at lunch. She was friendly.)

How would you feel if you were new and someone reached out the way Nicole did? (grateful, relieved, happy)

Amy misunderstood Nicole's intentions at first and felt hurt. What could have happened if Amy had acted on her misunderstanding? (Amy could have said things she didn't mean. The girls could have gotten angry. Amy and Nicole might not have become friends.)

What have we learned from Amy's situation? (Don't jump to conclusions. Ask what a person's intentions are. Give friends a chance to show you that their intentions are good.)

SKILL UNLOCKED!

How to stop jumping to conclusions

ASTOP

First, stop and ask yourself, “Is there a good way to explain these actions?”

BTALK

Next, talk to the other person and find out his or her intentions.

There may be different reasons for a person's actions. When you think you know the reason and don't check it out first, it's called jumping to conclusions.

But it's important to stop and give people a chance. Find out if there are good reasons for a person's actions.

Actions are something you can see. But it's hard to know what a person's intentions are unless you talk about them with him or her.
